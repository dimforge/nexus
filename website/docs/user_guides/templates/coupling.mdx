The `RbdCoupling` argument of `state.insert_rigid_body` lets MPM particles interact with a
[rigid-body collider](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/colliders/). This is typically used for a container
holding sand, or a moving blade pushing an elastic object. Use `BoundaryCondition` to choose how the material
moves wrt. the collider's surface.

## Coupling modes

There are three `RbdCoupling` modes:

- <rust>`RbdCoupling::None`</rust><python>`RbdCoupling.NONE`</python> selects ordinary rigid-body dynamics, without
  an MPM boundary.
- <rust>`RbdCoupling::MpmOneWay(boundary)`</rust><python>`RbdCoupling.mpm_one_way(boundary)`</python> lets the body
  affect particles without receiving their reaction. This is typically used for fixed walls or prescribed motion.
- <rust>`RbdCoupling::MpmTwoWay(boundary)`</rust><python>`RbdCoupling.mpm_two_way(boundary)`</python> also transfers
  particle impulses to the body.

With <rust>`RbdCoupling::MpmTwoWay`</rust><python>`RbdCoupling.mpm_two_way`</python>, `pipeline.simulate` runs the
rigid-body step, then MPM integrates the coupled body's pose and writes it back. These separate updates don't resolve
all interactions simultaneously. Keep this limitation in mind when combining coupled bodies with
[articulated mechanisms](https://rapier.rs/docs/user_guides/rust/joint_constraints/) or ordinary rigid-body contacts.

## Boundary conditions

<rust>Import `BoundaryCondition` from `nexus2d::mpm::solver` or `nexus3d::mpm::solver`.</rust>
<python>Import `BoundaryCondition` from `nexus3d`.</python> Its constructors select the surface response:

- <rust>`BoundaryCondition::stick()`</rust><python>`BoundaryCondition.stick()`</python> removes relative velocity
  at the surface. The material follows the surface without sliding.
- <rust>`BoundaryCondition::slip()`</rust><python>`BoundaryCondition.slip()`</python> removes the normal component
  of relative velocity and preserves the tangential component.
- <rust>`BoundaryCondition::separate(friction)`</rust><python>`BoundaryCondition.separate(friction)`</python>
  prevents motion into the surface while allowing the material to move away. The `friction` argument opposes sliding.

## Compatible particle-in-cell

Nexus uses **CPIC** (Compatible Particle-In-Cell) by default. This lets transfers account for boundaries separating
particles from grid nodes. Call `state.set_mpm_use_cpic(enabled)` to change this setting. For a first simulation,
it is recommended to keep <rust>`enabled = true`</rust><python>`enabled = True`</python>.

## Environment and timestep

The `state.finalize` method collects coupled boundaries from rigid-body environment `0`.
Calling `state.add_environment` creates more rigid-body environments, without duplicating the MPM material or its
boundaries. Insert an MPM boundary with `state.insert_rigid_body`, or with `state.insert_rigid_body_in(0, ...)`.

The rigid-body and MPM solvers have independent timesteps. The MPM `SimulationParams.dt` must match the total
rigid-body time advanced by `pipeline.simulate` if the results need to stay synchronized.
<rust>For example, `RbdSimParams.dt = 1 / 120` with `state.set_rbd_steps_per_frame(2)` matches
`SimulationParams.dt = 1 / 60`.</rust>
<python>The default rigid-body timestep is `1 / 60`, so leave `state.set_rbd_steps_per_frame(1)` when using
`SimulationParams(..., 1 / 60)`.</python>
The `state.set_mpm_substeps` method subdivides that MPM time; it doesn't change the total `SimulationParams.dt`.

Call `state.set_rbd_gravity` after `state.finalize`. For the material, supply `gravity` in
<rust>`SimulationParams`</rust><python>`SimulationParams(gravity, dt)`</python>, or call `state.set_mpm_gravity`. Keep the two values consistent when gravity should affect both parts of the scene equally.

:::info
If particles pass through the ground, check its `RbdCoupling` value and environment index first. Then check `cell_width`,
`SimulationParams.dt`, and `state.set_mpm_substeps` as described in
[MPM timestep and gravity](./particles.mdx#timestep-and-gravity). A visible shape from `viewer.insert_shape` isn't
evidence that `state.finalize` registered an MPM boundary.
:::
